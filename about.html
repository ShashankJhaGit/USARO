About USARO

Applied Research, Real Impact

USARO is a national research competition designed to lead students into the real world by conducting applied research across multiple fields.

USARO is designed to bridge the gap between ambition and opportunity for students who want to engage in meaningful research. These students face real barriers — less access to research labs, mentorship with researchers, or funding to apply research to real-world problems. USARO is built to close that gap.

Competitions

Three formats, each testing a different dimension of research readiness.

Quarterly

Case Study Competitions

Competitors are presented with a scenario drawn from healthcare, computer science, engineering, economics, or public policy, and must analyze the available information, identify key challenges, and propose evidence-based solutions. Cases may involve ethical dilemmas, technical failures, public health crises, cybersecurity incidents, market disruptions, or engineering design challenges. Rather than rewarding memorization, these competitions evaluate a student's ability to interpret information, think critically, justify decisions with research, and communicate practical recommendations.

Skills Assessed
  • Critical thinking
  • Evidence-based decision making
  • Research interpretation
  • Problem solving
  • Written & verbal communication
Biannual

Research Knowledge Testing Events

These events assess a competitor's understanding of scientific literature, research methodology, experimental design, statistics, and discipline-specific concepts. Questions are developed from published academic papers and research principles rather than standard classroom curricula. Competitors are expected to analyze figures, interpret data, evaluate methodologies, and apply research concepts to unfamiliar situations. The highest-performing students receive national recognition and demonstrate exceptional readiness for advanced research opportunities.

Skills Assessed
  • Scientific literacy
  • Data interpretation
  • Experimental design
  • Statistical reasoning
  • Academic reading comprehension
Annual

Research Presentation Event

Students present either an original research project or a well-developed research proposal to a panel of university professors and research professionals, and are evaluated on the quality of their research, originality, communication skills, and ability to defend their ideas in a live Q&A. Outstanding presentations may lead to mentorship opportunities — guidance in expanding a project, conducting the proposed study, or preparing work for publication or conference presentation.

Skills Assessed
  • Research communication
  • Scientific reasoning
  • Presentation skills
  • Research methodology
  • Professional discussion

Accessibility & Competition Philosophy

USARO is built on the belief that exceptional researchers can come from any background. Every competition rewards curiosity, analytical thinking, creativity, and sound methodology — not access to expensive laboratories or institutional resources.

No Lab Required

Students are not expected to have access to university laboratories, specialized equipment, or institutional research programs. Many successful projects can be completed using publicly available datasets, computational methods, literature reviews, simulations, surveys, or theoretical analysis.

Skill Over Resources

Judging emphasizes the quality of a student's ideas, reasoning, methodology, and communication instead of the amount of funding, equipment, or mentorship available during the project. Competitors are rewarded for demonstrating strong research practices and thoughtful problem-solving.

Free & Digital-First

USARO competitions are conducted primarily online, allowing students from across the United States to participate without the financial burden of travel or registration costs — while maintaining rigorous academic standards and meaningful interactions with judges and mentors.
